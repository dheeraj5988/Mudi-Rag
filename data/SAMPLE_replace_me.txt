Influenza (flu) is a contagious respiratory illness caused by influenza viruses. Common symptoms include fever, cough, sore throat, runny or stuffy nose, muscle or body aches, headaches and fatigue. Symptoms usually begin suddenly. Most people recover in less than two weeks. Precautions include resting, drinking plenty of fluids, covering coughs, washing hands often and staying home while sick. People should seek urgent medical care for difficulty breathing, persistent chest pain, confusion, severe vomiting, or fever that returns or worsens after improving.

The common cold is a viral infection of the nose and throat. Symptoms include sneezing, runny nose, sore throat, mild cough and low-grade fever. Colds usually improve within seven to ten days. Rest and fluids help. See a doctor if symptoms last more than ten days or are severe.

Dengue is a mosquito-borne viral disease. Symptoms include high fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, joint and muscle pain, rash and nausea. Warning signs needing urgent care include severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding gums and rapid breathing. Prevention involves avoiding mosquito bites and removing standing water.
